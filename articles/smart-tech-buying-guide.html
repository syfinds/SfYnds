A smarter way to shop for tech.

Which specifications matter, what to ignore and how to compare products fairly.

Tech product pages can make every feature sound essential. A better approach is to start with how you will use the device, then compare a short list of relevant specifications and practical ownership details.

1. Check compatibility first

Confirm that the product works with your phone, computer, operating system, ports, apps and other equipment. Compatibility problems can turn a bargain into a return.

2. Compare features that affect everyday use

  • Battery: Look for independent or clearly described usage estimates where available.
  • Performance: Match processor, memory or other specifications to the tasks you actually do.
  • Connectivity: Check supported standards, range and required adapters.
  • Durability: Review materials, repair options and warranty terms.
  • Software support: For connected devices, consider updates, app requirements and support lifespan.

3. Look beyond the headline price

Include delivery, required accessories, subscription fees and potential replacement costs. Check the return policy and warranty from the retailer or manufacturer. Prices and offers can change, so verify them at the time of purchase.

4. Read reviews with care

Prefer detailed reviews that explain how a product was used and what its limitations are. Repeated comments across multiple sources can be more useful than one glowing or negative rating. Be cautious with vague reviews and claims that cannot be verified.

Comparison tip

Make a short table with your three most important requirements. Score every shortlisted product against the same criteria instead of comparing unrelated marketing claims.

5. Decide based on your use case

The right choice for gaming may be different from the right choice for travel or study. A higher specification is not automatically better value if you will never use it.
